# Code of Conduct

## The short version

Be civil. Argue about the code, not the person writing it. Assume the other
party is trying to help until they give you a reason to think otherwise.

## What is expected

- Keep criticism about the change: what it does, what it breaks, what it costs
  to maintain. "This allocates on every keystroke" is useful. "This is
  amateurish" is not.
- Accept that a maintainer may decline a change that works. A feature has an
  ongoing cost, and a project of this size can only carry so much.
- Accept a review that asks for more. The coverage gate and the comment
  density here are unusual on purpose, and holding a contribution to them is
  not personal.
- Assume good faith about someone's English, their tooling, their terminal, or
  how long they have been programming.

## What is not

Harassment, personal attacks, discrimination on any grounds, deliberate
disruption, publishing anyone's private information, or sustained argument
after a decision has been made and explained.

## Scope

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, discussions, and
anywhere someone is representing the project.

## Enforcement

Report a problem to the maintainer at
<https://github.com/MartinGallagher-code>, or by opening an issue if the
matter is public and minor. Reports are handled privately.

The maintainer may edit or delete comments, close issues and pull requests, or
block an account. What happens depends on what was done and whether it
continues after being raised; a first misstep is normally a word, not a ban.

## Attribution

This is a plain-language policy rather than an adaptation of the Contributor
Covenant, but it is meant to cover the same ground.
